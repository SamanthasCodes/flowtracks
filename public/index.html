Ocean Pollution Flow Tracker
Every minute, one garbage truck of plastic enters our oceans.
Once in the oceans, large circulation patterns (hundreds of kilometers) transport plastic across
long distances while smaller turbulent motions (hundreds of meters) redistribute particles through the surrounding water,
shaping how widely plastic disperses. Together, these dynamics make it difficult to track plastic's fate.

FlowTracks
ocean pollution flow tracker

FlowTracks simulates buoyant plastic fragments carried by ocean surface currents and the marine plastic debris introduced into the ocean in 2025.


The choropleth colors each country by its projected annual plastic marine debris in 2025 (tonnes per year), so you can immediately see which nations contribute the most. The simulation lets you click any ocean region to release virtual particles that drift with real ocean currents, showing how plastic pollution can travel far from its source and spread unpredictably through turbulence.


Each dot on the map represents a single piece of plastic—a bottle forgotten at the beach, a net a fisherman lost, a straw tossed overboard. To make the scale tangible, here is what one ton of common ocean debris looks like:
