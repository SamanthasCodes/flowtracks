Where does my trash go?

Plastic is one of the most widespread environmental pollutants on Earth. Its production has grown so rapidly that disposal and recycling systems cannot keep up. Each year, an estimated 5 to 13 million tons of plastic enter the ocean1, yet only about 300,000 tons can currently be accounted for in observations2. A major reason for this discrepancy is the difficulty of measuring and tracking plastic debris in the marine environment3.

While large-scale ocean circulation (spanning hundreds of kilometers) largely determines where plastic accumulates, smaller-scale turbulent motions (on the order of kilometers or less) control how widely the particles disperse. Predicting this spreading remains a major scientific challenge and an active area of research.

This simulation lets you release a cloud of virtual plastic particles and watch how ocean dynamics shape their movement. By adjusting turbulence and other parameters, you can explore how small-scale ocean dynamics influence the particles' large-scale distribution and ultimate fate.

Before you begin
How to use FlowTracks
Click anywhere on the ocean to release a cloud of particles. Each one represents a piece of plastic. Use the controls to change how the simulation behaves.
Particles
How many plastic pieces to simulate at once. More particles gives a more accurate picture of dispersal.
Speed
How fast time moves in the simulation. Increase it to watch years of ocean drift unfold in seconds.
Diffusion
How much small-scale turbulence scatters particles off the main current. Higher values produce a wider, messier spread.
Fade opacity
How long particle trails linger on screen. Near 1.0 preserves the full history; lower values show only recent movement.
Drop rate
How often new particles are added at the source, simulating ongoing pollution input over time.
